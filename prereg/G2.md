## LeWM differences acknowledged (from `notes/lewm_diff.md`)

Our model is a stand-in, not LeWM. The differences most likely to matter:

1. **Action conditioning.**
   - LeWM uses AdaLN-zero: zero output at init, but O(1) gradient on the modulation.
   - Ours uses FiLM with random init (act_init_scale 1.0, so the action pathway is "on" at init), and the history and copy-forward paths are fully on.
   - In the linear theory's terms (`notes/theory.md` Result 3), both are *fast* action pathways: neither needs the encoder to carry the agent before V can move. So the slow-pathway mechanism of P1 is not what G2 tests.
2. **SIGReg.**
   - Same Epps-Pulley form, but ours pools time steps, uses 256 directions, sums the prediction loss over d, and applies BatchNorm to the latent itself.
   - Our effective weight at sigreg_w 0.1 is 3.6 to 14 times weaker than LeWM's 0.09. The LeWM-equivalent value is 0.09·d/(h+1).
   - **Decision:** the whitening rule below (the default route). LeWM's exact SIGReg is not implemented here, so its weight cannot simply be transferred. We report the chosen weight next to the LeWM-equivalent one.
3. **Architecture and data.**
   - CNN encoder rather than ViT-tiny.
   - d 16 or 32 rather than 192.
   - Residual predictor rather than LN and an MLP projector.
   - h 1 rather than 3, with no frame skip (LeWM: 5).
   - AdamW at constant lr 3e-4 rather than 5e-5 with cosine decay.

SLIM (2610.03137, appendix H, verified) found that the SIGReg weight does not cause the collapse in LeWM (0.03, 0.09 and 0.27 all fail).

## Calibration (g2_calib)

- **Purpose:** choose the SIGReg weight by whitening quality only, before looking at agent metrics.
- **Rule:** the smallest `sigreg_w` in {0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1.0} whose IDM-always run (5000 steps, seed 0) ends with both:
  - cov_err < 0.15;
  - 0.5d ≤ z_var ≤ 2d.
- **Applied automatically by** `gates.py g2_calib`. The chosen value goes to every g2_reproduce run via `--override`.

## Reproduce (g2_reproduce)

- **Ladder order:** (set, 2, 16), (set, 2, 32), (set, 4, 16), (set, 4, 32), (integrator, 2, 16), (integrator, 2, 32), (integrator, 4, 16), (integrator, 4, 32), as (agent_mode, n_dist, d).
- **Rule:** the first config where seed 0 has plain final agent_r2 < 0.5 and IDM-always ≥ 0.8. It is written to `runs/g2/CHOSEN_CONFIG.json` by `gates.py g2_reproduce`.
- **If none qualifies:** the pixel arm is NO-GO.
- **Notes:**
  - The linear smoke tests predict a failure for the "set" agent. The "integrator" agent tests P6 (pixel-pattern decorrelation).
  - **Theory note (added before running):** in the linear theory, a trap needs a mismatch between the agent's own dynamics and those of the competing factors. A "set" agent (d ≈ 0) competing with smoothly moving distractors fits this.
  - **Cost:** g2_calib and g2_reproduce also serve candidate #3 (G4b needs tiny models on the chosen config).

## Decisive test (g2_trap, seeds 0 to 2)

**Primary metric:** final agent_r2 (linear probe of agent position from z_t; ground truth from the simulator).

| Outcome | Condition |
|---|---|
| **PASS** | mean warm-up ≥ 0.8 × mean IDM-always, and every seed ≥ 0.7 × that seed's IDM-always |
| **KILL** | mean warm-up ≤ mean plain + 0.1 |
| **INCONCLUSIVE** | mean plain ≥ 0.5, or mean IDM-always < 0.8 |

**Secondary:** planning success with `plan_eval.py` defaults (100 episodes, H 5, S 256, E 32, 3 iterations, tolerance 0.06, seed 0). PASS-secondary if warm-up ≥ 0.8 × IDM-always.

**Warm-up:** the first 5% of steps, IDM weight 1.0 (TrainConfig defaults). If G1's dose sweep shows a larger dose is needed, change it *here, before* running g2_trap, and record the reason.

**Hold (8 Nov 2026):** g2_trap is not launched until the user has seen the Stage 0 collision finding (OpenReview t6sUZeEbkt, `notes/collision_check.md`).
