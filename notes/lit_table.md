| Keeping JEPA World Models Plannable When Little of the Frame Moves (Strohm, Wagner, Schwab, Huber) | 2610.03137 | [V] full text | 8 Nov | Phenomenon. | Preserving Unstable Modes Through Inverse Dynamics in JEPA World Models (Toso, LeCun, Anderson, Bounou) | 2610.07540 | [V] full text | 8 Nov | Objective level only. Lemma 2 is an existence result: for some unstable linear systems and data distributions, the prediction loss and SIGReg are both minimised by an encoder whose kernel contains every unstable mode. Theorem 1: exact endpoint IDM (EP-IDM) makes the encoder injective on the H-step reachable subspace. Theorem 2: as H grows, the top eigenspace of the finite-horizon controllability Gramian converges to the unstable subspace. They do not analyse training dynamics, which minimum gradient descent reaches, or any IDM schedule, annealing or warm-up. The IDM is trained jointly at a constant weight for the whole run; in the main method EP-IDM replaces SIGReg ("in lieu of"). The only warm-up is for the learning rate. Also submitted to ICLR 2027 (OpenReview ei8NxUoCD5) | "the training loss can be minimized while these modes are collapsed"; "More than just constructing those sufficient representations, we also ask whether standard JEPA training can learn them in the first place."; "We jointly optimize the encoders, predictor, and inverse dynamics model"; "The coefficients of the active prediction, regularization, and action-reconstruction objectives are set to one."; "The learning rate is linearly warmed up during the first five epochs and then follows a cosine schedule." |
